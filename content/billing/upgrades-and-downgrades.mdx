---
title: "Upgrading or downgrading a server"
description: "Move your ATBP server to a bigger or smaller plan, or between Budget+ and Premium+, from the billing area. Your files move with it."
---

Your service page on https://billing.atbphosting.com (Services > your server) shows its actions as buttons at the top right: **Renew**, a red **Cancel service**, **Upgrade**, **Downgrade** and, when you can still ask for one, **Refund**. **Manage Server** is under **Quick actions**.

![A billing service page: the Renew, Cancel service, Upgrade, Downgrade and Refund buttons at the top right, with Manage Server under Quick actions](/screenshots/billing-service.webp)

The **Upgrade** and **Downgrade** buttons do this:

- **Upgrade** shows bigger plans in your line, plus plans on the other line (Budget+ or Premium+) that cost more and have at least your RAM. On the next step, Splits and Extra Backups can stay the same or go up.
- **Downgrade** shows smaller plans in your line, plus plans on the other line that cost less. A cheaper Budget+ plan can have more RAM than your Premium+ plan; it still shows here, because it costs less. On the next step, Splits and Extra Backups can stay the same or go down.

A plan of the same size that costs less (for example an old Starter Bot to Bot 1 GB) shows under **Downgrade**, and one that costs more under **Upgrade**.

A button only appears when there is somewhere to go, and only while the service is paid up: if your server has an unpaid invoice, pay it first and then upgrade.

How the page is laid out:

- The title says what you are changing, for example **Upgrade Premium+ 4 GB · 1.5 vCPU**. When your plan has options (Splits, Extra Backups), two steps show at the top right: **Plan**, then **Options**.
- **Your plan** is at the top with what you pay today. Pick it to keep your plan and only change Splits or Extra Backups.
- Plans are grouped by line, one card each. Your own line comes first (for example **Stay on Premium+**), then any other line (for example **Move to Budget+**). In each group the plan nearest to what you pay now comes first. Each group shows its lowest price per GB of RAM, so you can compare Budget+ and Premium+ at a glance, and a group on another line says once that your server moves there.
- Each card shows the plan's RAM change (for example **+6 GB RAM**), its price and the difference from what you pay for your plan now, for example **+₱450.00 per month** or **−₱150.00 per month**. Splits and Extra Backups are not in that difference, because they stay as they are.
- The summary (**Upgrade summary** or **Downgrade summary**) shows your current plan, the new plan and what you pay today (**Total Today**) or get back as credit (**Credit back**) for the days left. It stays on screen while you scroll; on a phone, the amount and the button sit in a bar at the bottom of the screen.

To upgrade:

1. Go to Services > your server > **Upgrade**.
2. Pick the plan and press **Next Step**.

   ![The Upgrade page, step 1 Plan: Your plan at the top, a Stay on Premium+ group and a Move to Budget+ group of plan cards with their RAM change, price and difference per month, one plan picked, and the Upgrade summary with Total Today and the Next Step button](/screenshots/billing-upgrade.webp)

3. On **Raise your options**, choose your Splits and Extra Backups. The summary shows **Total Today**; press **Upgrade**. If your plan has no options, there is no second step and the button on the first step is **Upgrade**.

   ![The Upgrade summary on step 2: Current Plan, New Plan, New plan 20 of 30 days, Unused time on current plan, Total Today and the Upgrade button](/screenshots/billing-upgrade-summary.webp)

4. Pay the invoice with your preferred [payment method](/billing/payment-methods). The server upgrades once the invoice is marked paid.
5. Restart the server in the panel so the new resources apply.

To downgrade:

1. Make sure your files fit the smaller storage first.
2. Go to Services > your server > **Downgrade**, pick the plan and press **Next Step**.

   ![The Downgrade page, step 1 Plan: smaller Premium+ plans and cheaper Budget+ plans with the difference per month, one plan picked, and the Downgrade summary with Credit back and the Next Step button](/screenshots/billing-downgrade.webp)

3. On **Lower your options**, choose your Splits and Extra Backups. The summary shows the **Credit back**; press **Downgrade**. If your plan has no options, press **Downgrade** on the first step.
4. The change applies right away and the unused difference goes to your account credit. Restart the server in the panel.

How the credit is worked out:

- You get back what you actually paid for the days left. If your first month was bought with a coupon (for example 25% off), those days are credited at the price you paid, not the full price.
- Free days added to your service (for example after an outage) are not credited.
- If a plan change is waiting for payment and you pay your renewal first, the plan change is cancelled, because its price was for the old end date. Open Upgrade again to get a price for the new period.

## Moving between Budget+ and Premium+

Every game (Minecraft, Hytale, Palworld, FiveM, Terraria and Valheim) can move between Budget+ and Premium+ in both directions. Budget+ and Premium+ run on different machines. When you pick a plan on the other line, your server moves there by itself: after the invoice is paid for an upgrade, or straight away for a downgrade.

A plan on the other line can stay on your Upgrade or Downgrade page even when it is sold out for new orders. It shows when a machine on that line can really take your server, judged on what your server uses. If it costs more than you pay now it is on Upgrade, even when it has less RAM; if it costs less it is on Downgrade.

Split servers move with the main server, one after the other, so each is offline while its files copy and gets a new address and port. A plan's RAM, storage and CPU cover the main server and its splits together. If your split servers would not fit the new plan, or you have more of them than it allows, delete or shrink them in the panel first; the page tells you which.

- Your files, worlds and settings come with it. Your backups do not: they stay behind on the old machine and no longer show in the panel. Make a new backup once the move is done, and download any older backup you want to keep before you change plans.
- It gets a new address and port. The new ones show in the panel once the move is done. A subdomain you made in the panel follows the server to its new address by itself when the move lands. Players' devices can take a few minutes to see the change.
- Extra ports get new numbers too. If a plugin or mod uses one (for example a voice chat), set its new port in that plugin's config.
- Bedrock crossplay (GeyserMC) is set up for you: Geyser gets its own Bedrock port, which is a new one on Premium+, and its config is updated to use it. Bedrock players connect with your address and that port, which shows in the panel. It applies from the next start.
- It stops first. A server still running about 4 minutes later is force stopped so the move can go ahead.
- It is offline for a few minutes while the files are copied, longer for very large servers. Your server's page shows how far the copy is, in percent.
- If the move fails, or cannot start because a reinstall or backup restore is running, it tries again by itself, up to three more times over about 2.5 hours.
- The new plan's limits apply as soon as it lands. If the new plan has less RAM and the move has to wait, the smaller limits apply right away. Start the server again from the panel if it does not come back on its own.

Old Budget Micro servers can move up to Budget+ or Premium+ (6 GB and bigger) from the Upgrade button, and they move to the new machines the same way. Budget Micro is retired and cannot be renewed, so this is the way to keep a Budget Micro server past the end of its term; the upgraded server renews as normal. No plan can move back to old Budget.

## Upgrading to switch games

Every game costs the same per GB, but each has a smallest plan. When your plan is too small for a game, the panel's **Settings > Change Game** shows what it needs and an **Upgrade** button. That button opens the Upgrade page with only the plans big enough for that game. Pay the difference like any other upgrade, then pick the game in Change Game.

A plan change also keeps your current game in mind: a smaller plan that is below your game's smallest size (for example a 4 GB plan for a Hytale server) is refused. Switch games first, or pick a bigger plan.

## Older Premium+ plans where you picked the RAM and CPU

Premium+ servers bought before the current plans have their RAM and CPU set by you at checkout. Those amounts can no longer be changed on that plan, but everything else still works:

- **Splits and server software** can still be changed on the same plan. Your RAM and CPU stay exactly as they are, and you only pay for what you changed.
- **To change size, move to one of the current [Premium+ plans](/plans/premium-plus-plans-and-prices).** They show on the Upgrade and Downgrade pages. Which page a plan is on depends on its price compared with what you pay today, not only on its RAM: a current plan that costs more is an Upgrade, one that costs less is a Downgrade.
- The move stays on the same machines, so your address, port and files do not change. The new plan's RAM, CPU, storage and limits apply as soon as it is done.
- Moving to a current plan is one way: you cannot go back to the older plan afterwards.
- The price is worked out the same way as any other change (below): what you pay today, including your RAM and CPU, is credited for the days left.


## How the price is worked out

The summary on the Upgrade or Downgrade page shows two lines before the total:

- **New plan, 21 of 31 days** (with your own numbers): what the new plan and options cost per day, times the days left until your due date.
- **Unused time on current plan**: what you are billed per day today, times the same days. This counts your term discount, any coupon and any paid Splits or Extra Backups, including ones the new plan does not have.

The total is the first line minus the second. Days are counted on the real length of your billing cycle (31 days for a 31-day month), so a day costs the same whichever month you are in.

Example: a Budget+ 6 GB server at ₱210 a month, due on 31 October, moves to Premium+ 6 GB (₱450) on 10 October. 21 of the cycle's 31 days are left. The new plan line is ₱450 / 31 x 21 = ₱304.84, unused time is ₱210 / 31 x 21 = ₱142.26, so the upgrade invoice is ₱162.58. Moving straight back the same day returns ₱162.58 as account credit.

- Your due date stays the same; the next renewal is at the new plan's price. The total you pay today covers only the days left, and it is higher the earlier you upgrade in your cycle.
- **Downgrades** return the difference as account credit, used on your next invoice.
- If you renewed several months ahead at a discount, the days you prepaid keep that discount on both sides: unused time is credited at the rate you paid, and the new plan runs on those days at the same discount.
- A change made on your due date costs nothing today; the new price starts with the next invoice.
- An unpaid upgrade invoice is re-priced every day, so it always matches the days still left when you pay.
- An upgrade on the same tier grows your server on the machine it is already on, so the Upgrade page offers a bigger plan only when that machine has room for it. Stock on the order page is for new servers on any machine, so a plan can show in stock there and still be missing from your Upgrade page while your machine is full. It also works the other way: some servers can move up to a size that is sold out for new orders, depending on what the server really uses and how much room its machine has (for example, a bigger plan with the same vCPU). What the Upgrade page offers can change from day to day, and for a while during the day when your machine is very busy. A smaller plan on the same machines is always offered on the Downgrade page.
- An unpaid upgrade invoice changes nothing: your server stays on its current plan and your next renewal stays at the current price until you pay it.
- While an upgrade invoice is unpaid you cannot start another change, and the Upgrade page will not open. Pay that invoice first. If you picked the wrong plan and the invoice has no cancel option, open a ticket on our [Discord](https://discord.atbphosting.com). If your Discord is linked to billing, Mari can cancel the unpaid upgrade invoice there after you reply yes, as long as nothing has been paid on it. Otherwise staff remove it; you can leave it unpaid in the meantime.
